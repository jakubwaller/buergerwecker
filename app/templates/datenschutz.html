What data is stored?

Email, city, language (de/en), chosen filters, timestamps (including when a separate consent under Art. 9 was given, where applicable). If a mail to you fails permanently or you mark one as spam, we also store that fact, the reason your mail provider gave, and the date, so we stop mailing that address — legal basis Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in not sending mail that cannot be delivered. A delivery failure is deleted on the same schedule as the subscription itself. If you mark one of our emails as spam, we keep your address, that fact and the date for one year, so that we do not mail you again in the meantime. You can ask us to remove that entry sooner at the address below, which also makes you contactable again. We store no IP address and no name (the exceptions: the app’s pseudonymised network identifier kept for about an hour, described under “The app”; and the contact form, which keeps the IP address in memory for about an hour and takes the optional name you enter, see “Contact form”). We set no tracking cookies; our CDN (Cloudflare) may process your IP address and set a strictly-necessary anti-bot cookie to deliver and protect the site — no profiling, no consent required.

Legal basis

Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR — consent via double opt-in.

Sensitive appointment types (Art. 9 GDPR)

For a few appointment types, the mere act of selecting one reveals a special category of personal data — for example counselling on sexually transmitted infections (health data) or a declaration under the Self-Determination Act (data about gender identity). Where we list such an appointment type, we ask for a separate, explicit consent on the sign-up form; the legal basis is then Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR in addition to Art. 6(1)(a). Without that consent no such subscription is created.

These subscriptions are treated differently: they expire after {{ sensitive_ttl_days }} days instead of {{ ttl_days }}, and our notification emails name neither the appointment type nor the office — only that a slot is free, plus date, time and a neutral booking link. You can withdraw the consent at any time with the unsubscribe link in every email, which deletes the subscription (Art. 7(3) GDPR).

Retention period

Subscriptions expire automatically {{ ttl_days }} days after sign-up — {{ sensitive_ttl_days }} days for the sensitive appointment types described above — unless you confirm, via the link in the “still looking?” e-mail we send shortly before, that you are still looking. An expired subscription is paused for {{ grace_days }} days (no notifications; the same link can switch it back on), then deleted. Deleted records are permanently removed 30 days after deletion. The single exception is a spam complaint, described above, which we keep for one year. We keep daily backups of the database for 30 days, so anything we remove, including data you delete in the app, is gone from the last backup 30 days later at the latest.

The app

The Bürgerwecker app for iPhone and Android notifies you by push notification instead of email. It needs no email address and no name. We store the device’s push token (the identifier Apple or Google assign so that notifications reach this installation), the platform (iOS or Android), the language, a hashed access key for the app, timestamps (registration, last use, confirmation), and for each alert the same as for an email subscription: city, chosen filters, timestamps and, where applicable, when the separate consent under Art. 9 was given. Before the first alert, the server sends a test notification with a one-time code that the app confirms; only a hash of the code is stored, and the code is valid for 24 hours.

Notifications are delivered through the Apple Push Notification service (Apple) on iPhone and through Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google) on Android. These services receive the push token and the content of the notification: city, offices, dates and times of free slots and a booking link — for the sensitive appointment types described above only how many slots are free, with a neutral link; the “still looking?” question for them names only the city and the date the alert ends. Apple and Google may also process this data in the USA; Apple bases such transfers on Standard Contractual Clauses, and Google LLC is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, your consent by allowing notifications and setting up an alert in the app; for sensitive appointment types additionally Art. 9(2)(a) GDPR via the separate consent in the app. You withdraw it by deleting the alert, or everything at once with “Delete my data” in the app’s settings, which removes the device record and all its alerts immediately.

To limit automated use of the app, we count requests per network and per device. For each registration, token change or request for a new test notification, we keep a keyed hash of the network it came from (the IPv4 address, or for IPv6 the network prefix, at most the first half of the address) — never the address itself — with the time and, for a new push token, the device it belongs to; we also note when a device fetches free slots. These entries are deleted after about an hour. In addition, we count the test notifications sent per push token under a hash of the token; these are deleted after one to two days. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in protecting the service and the cities’ booking sites from misuse.

On Android, the app asks Google Play for an integrity verdict on the app and the device when it registers (Google’s Play Integrity API), to keep scripted fake devices out. Our server sends the resulting token to Google to have it decoded, so Google receives it; Google LLC may process it in the USA and is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework. We use only the verdict — whether this is the genuine app on a genuine device — and store nothing of it, except that a failed check is counted under the network hash described above for ten minutes. Reading the information on the phone that the check needs is strictly necessary for the registration you ask for (§ 25(2) no. 2 TDDDG). Legal basis for the processing: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in protecting the service from automated, fake registrations.

When you answer the “still looking?” question with “No, I’ve got one”, or stop an alert that has notified you of free slots (according to the notification list on your phone), the app asks the operating system to show the rating dialog of the App Store or Google Play. Whether it appears is up to Apple or Google; the app learns neither whether it appeared nor whether or how you rate, stores nothing for it and sends nothing to our server for it. A rating goes directly to Apple or Google, each independently responsible for it under its own privacy policy.

Alerts in the app run for the same terms as email subscriptions; the “still looking?” question arrives as a notification. A device record is deleted 30 days after its last alert has ended, at the earliest 30 days after the app was last used; a device that never confirmed the test notification and holds no alert is deleted after one day. The home-screen widget fetches the free slots for the cities of your alerts from our server with the app’s access key; alerts for sensitive appointment types never appear in it.

Until the Android app is publicly available, you can test it in advance by joining our Google Group “buergerwecker-beta”. Google and we, as the group’s owner, then see the email address of your Google account; other members do not. We use it only to give you access to the test release on Google Play, and we delete the group once the app is publicly available there. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, your consent by joining; you withdraw it by leaving the group.

Contact form

The contact form (/kontakt) is also used by other projects of the same operator — Bundesschnitt, CrashGap, PapaMap and Zapfkompass — whose imprints and privacy notices link here. If you write to us through it, we process the name you give (optional), your email address, your message, the language and the project you chose, solely to answer your enquiry. Legal basis: Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in answering enquiries and preventing misuse; where the enquiry concerns a contract or pre-contractual steps, Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR. To limit misuse we keep your IP address in the server’s memory for the rate limit for about an hour; it is removed at the next cleanup run, at the latest when the service restarts, and never written to disk or a database. A hidden form field serves as a further bot check.

The message is delivered by email to our mailbox through Mailjet, Brevo or Sweego (see “Processors”; the first available provider is used). To avoid a double submission arriving twice, the database keeps a hash of your address and the message for 14 days — not the text itself. The email stays in our mailbox for as long as the enquiry requires and is then deleted.

Deleting your data

In the app, “Delete my data” in the settings deletes the device record and all its alerts at once; a single alert you delete on its own. If you have already removed the app, its device record is deleted automatically on the terms described under “The app”. An email subscription is deleted with the unsubscribe link in every email. How long deleted data stays in the database and its backups is described under “Retention period”. For anything else, such as the spam record described above, an email to jakub@jakubwaller.eu is enough.

Your rights

Access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), portability (Art. 20), withdrawal of consent (Art. 7(3)). You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority (Art. 77). Contact: jakub@jakubwaller.eu.

Right to object

Where we process data on the basis of our legitimate interest (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) — the records of failed deliveries and spam complaints, the app’s counts against misuse, the contact form’s rate limit and the Android integrity check described above — you have the right to object to this processing at any time on grounds relating to your particular situation (Art. 21 GDPR). An email to jakub@jakubwaller.eu is enough.

Die App

Vor dem ersten Alarm schickt der Server eine Test-Mitteilung mit einem Einmal-Code, den die App bestätigt; gespeichert wird nur ein Hash des Codes, und der Code gilt 24 Stunden.

Die Mitteilungen werden auf dem iPhone über den Apple Push Notification service (Apple) und unter Android über Firebase Cloud Messaging (Google) zugestellt. Diese Dienste erhalten das Push-Token und den Inhalt der Mitteilung: Stadt, Ämter, Daten und Uhrzeiten freier Termine und einen Buchungslink — bei den oben beschriebenen besonders geschützten Anliegen nur, wie viele Termine frei sind, mit einem neutralen Link; die „Suchst du noch?“-Frage nennt bei ihnen nur die Stadt und das Ablaufdatum des Alarms. Um eine automatisierte Nutzung der App zu begrenzen, zählen wir Anfragen je Netz und je Gerät. Diese Einträge werden nach etwa einer Stunde gelöscht. Zusätzlich zählen wir die gesendeten Test-Mitteilungen je Push-Token unter einem Hash des Tokens; diese werden nach ein bis zwei Tagen gelöscht. Unter Android fragt die App bei der Registrierung bei Google Play ein Integritätsurteil über App und Gerät ab (Play Integrity API von Google), um automatisierte Fake-Geräte fernzuhalten. Unser Server schickt das daraus entstehende Token zur Auswertung an Google; Google erhält es also.
